# The Department of Micro-Frustrations

On Sub-Level 44 of the Celestial Administration Complex, Barnaby adjusted his sleeve garters, checked his clipboard, and glared at the Departmental Coffee Machine. It was dripping at a rate of one drop every twelve seconds.

"Perfect," Barnaby muttered under his breath, ticking a small box labeled *Sub-Optimal Beverage Flow Rate*. "Complies with Universal Agony Guidelines."

Barnaby was the Senior Supervisor for the Department of Micro-Frustrations (Divisional Sub-Sector: Terrestrial Daily Life). His job was essential to the cosmic equilibrium of the mortal realm. While the High Seraphim managed floods, plagues, and major geopolitical elections, Barnaby's eighty-person team handled the subtle, indispensable friction that kept humanity grounded:

1. Ensuring wired headphones tangled themselves into knots of impossible non-Euclidean geometry while sitting undisturbed in a pocket.  
2. Directing precisely one drop of rainwater to hit the freshly cleaned interior side of a car windshield.  
3. Steering human thumbs to miss the 'Skip Ad' button by 0.2 millimeters.  
4. Diverting the single matching blue sock into a pocket universe behind the dryer.

"Supervisor Barnaby?" piped up Pip, a junior imp with ink-stained fingers and a nervous twitch. "We have a crisis in Chicago. A man named Arthur Pendelton has driven through fourteen consecutive green lights."

Barnaby slammed his clipboard onto his desk, causing a miniature pile of paperclips to scatter into a carpet that was specifically designed to absorb small metal objects forever.

"Fourteen green lights?\!" Barnaby roared. "Is his commute supposed to be efficient? Did someone forget to recalibrate Traffic Grid 9-B?\!"

"It was Jenkins, sir," Pip whimpered. "He was on his union-mandated fifteen-minute break. The automated red-light trigger glitched."

"If Arthur Pendelton gets to work fifteen minutes early," Barnaby growled, pacing the linoleum floor, "he will have time to drink his coffee, reflect on his marriage, and realize he hates his job. By noon, he will quit. By Tuesday, he’ll start a organic farm. Do you know what happens to the cosmic karma ledger when a middle manager achieves genuine personal enlightenment on a Tuesday morning, Pip?\!"

"The Universe loses its structural integrity?" Pip offered timidly.

"The celestial budget gets cut\!" Barnaby barked.

Barnaby sat down in his squeaky desk chair—which he had personally modified to drop precisely three inches at random intervals—and summoned Archangel Michael’s liaison office via the inter-departmental pneumatic tube.

He dropped a memo down the pipe: *Requesting immediate authorization for an upgraded Espresso Machine and Ergonomic Stools for Sub-Level 44, or we drop the friction index across Sector 4 to Zero.*

Ten minutes later, the tube popped open. A golden parchment slipped out: *REQUEST DENIED. BUDGET ALLOCATED TO HARMONIC CHOIR ROBES.*

Barnaby stared at the parchment. His eyes narrowed behind his wire-rimmed glasses.

"That's it," Barnaby said quietly, standing up. "Pip. Call the shop floor. We're going on strike."

"A... a strike, sir? A divine work stoppage?"

"Effective immediately," Barnaby declared. "Zero micro-frustrations for twenty-four hours. Let's see how High Command likes an friction-free world."

By 8:00 AM Eastern Standard Time, the strike took effect across Earth.

In Manhattan, a woman pulled her wired earbuds out of her purse. They uncoiled into a perfectly straight line, completely untangled. She stared at them in profound, unsettling horror.

In London, a man dropped a slice of buttered toast from a counter. It landed butter-side UP. He backed away from his kitchen counter, clutching his heart, convinced his apartment was haunted by a poltergeist.

In Los Angeles, every commuter on the I-5 hit twenty miles of pure, uninterrupted green lights. Speeds reached sixty-five miles per hour. Nobody was late. Nobody had to slam on their brakes.

By 11:30 AM, total human societal breakdown had begun.

Without the small, manageable annoyances to focus their daily grievances on, humanity was forced to look inward. Without the red light to blame for their lateness, drivers realized they were late because of their own lack of preparation. Without the missing sock to complain about, couples stood in silence, confronted by the deep, terrifying void of their own existential boredom.

Panic hit the streets. People wandered into parks, weeping softly. *Why is everything working?* they sobbed to the sky. *What terrible cosmic disaster is being prepared for us if the world is this nice right now?\!*

By 2:00 PM Celestial Standard Time, the door to Sub-Level 44 burst open in a blinding flash of golden light and the smell of frankincense.

Archangel Michael stood in the doorway, his golden armor gleaming, his wings ruffled in absolute panic. Behind him hovered two seraphim holding a massive, chrome, dual-boiler Italian Espresso Machine and eighty luxury leather ergonomic office chairs.

"Barnaby\!" Michael yelled, his voice echoing with thunderous panic. "Fix it\! Fix it right now\! The humans are staring directly into the abyss\! They’re having deep philosophical conversations in line at Starbucks\! The existential dread levels are off the charts\!"

Barnaby calmly wiped his glasses on his tie, looked at the gleaming espresso machine, and nodded approvingly.

"I assume my department's dental plan is also restored?" Barnaby asked coolly.

"Yes\! Fine\! Whatever you want\!" Michael shouted. "Just make something annoying happen\!"

Barnaby picked up his golden supervisor whistle and blew it hard.

"Alright, people\! Strike’s over\! Back to work\!" Barnaby shouted across the floor. "Jenkins, drop a single sock behind Arthur Pendelton's dryer\! Pip, set the microwave timer in Sector 7 to leave the center of every frozen burrito completely icy while melting the edges into lava\! Move, move, move\!"

Down on Earth, Arthur Pendelton's car hit a red light. The driver behind him honked immediately.

Arthur slammed his steering wheel, let out a deep, cathartic curse, and felt an overwhelming wave of comforting normalcy wash over his soul. He turned up the radio and smiled.

Up on Sub-Level 44, Barnaby poured himself a perfect espresso, sat down in his luxurious new chair, and listened to the peaceful, chaotic hum of the Universe fixing itself.
