# The Tide of Whispers

In the village of Oakhaven, the ocean did not leave behind seashells, kelp, or driftwood when the tide receded. It left behind secrets.

Every morning before dawn, Elias put on his heavy rubber boots, slung a canvas sack over his shoulder, and walked down the cliffside steps to the grey, misty beach. He was Oakhaven’s designated Gleaner, a solitary position he had inherited from his father, and his father before him.

The wet sand was already littered with them.

Secrets in Oakhaven took on physical forms, spit out by the sea, born from the unspoken thoughts of the villagers sleeping in the cottages above. Most of them were ugly, heavy things. Elias knelt and picked up a rusted iron padlock wrapped in barbed wire. It weighed heavily in his gloved palm, practically humming with bitter resentment. *An unspoken grudge between brothers,* he guessed, tossing it into his sack.

A few feet away lay a shattered porcelain teacup, its edges stained with black ink. *A stolen inheritance. A lie told to a dying mother.* Into the sack it went.

If Elias didn’t gather them before the morning sun broke the horizon, the heat would melt the objects into a low, creeping fog. That fog would drift up into the village, seeping under doorways and through window cracks, infecting the town with paranoia, sudden rages, and inexplicable sorrows. It was his duty to carry the heavy sack to the edge of the jagged eastern peninsula and cast the burdens into the Abyssal Rift—a bottomless oceanic trench that swallowed the town's sins whole.

It was a lonely life. The villagers treated Elias with a mixture of reverence and deep, uncomfortable suspicion. No one looked him in the eye at the market, terrified he might somehow know which rusted nail or broken mirror belonged to them.

"Just the usual refuse today," Elias muttered to himself, his breath pluming in the cold air as he picked up a wooden spool of tangled, thorny twine. *Infidelity.*

He was turning toward the cliffside path when a flash of light caught his eye. It was resting in a small tide pool, half-buried in the sand.

Elias approached cautiously. He had seen thousands of secrets in his sixty years—most were sharp, dark, and heavy. But this object was a smooth, flawless sphere of sea-glass, radiating a soft, warm, bioluminescent violet light. It was beautiful.

He took off his thick leather glove and reached into the freezing water. The moment his bare skin touched the glass, a rush of sensation washed over him. It wasn't a feeling of guilt or malice. It was a feeling of profound, overwhelming peace, mixed with the quiet ache of a selfless sacrifice.

The secret unfolded in his mind like a blooming flower: *I knew the illness was returning, but I didn't tell him. I wanted our last summer together to be nothing but joy. I took the pain in silence so he could have the sunlight.*

Elias gasped, dropping to his knees in the wet sand. His heart hammered against his ribs.

The secret didn't belong to a villager. It belonged to Elara, his wife, who had passed away three years ago in the height of August. She had always told him it was a sudden, unexpected failure of her heart. She had lied. She had borne the agonizing knowledge of her failing health for months, shielding him from the impending grief so they could dance in the kitchen, tend to their garden, and sit on the porch without the shadow of death hanging over them.

Tears, hot and sudden, spilled down Elias's weathered cheeks, mixing with the salt spray of the ocean. He held the violet sphere to his chest. It was warm, like a stone left out in the midday sun.

He looked toward the eastern peninsula, where the Abyssal Rift waited for its daily sacrifice. He felt the heavy canvas sack of rust, thorns, and shattered glass cutting into his shoulder. Those belonged in the dark.

But this? This was not a burden to be discarded. It was a gift that the ocean had finally decided to return.

The sun began to crest the horizon, casting long golden rays across the water. Elias did not throw the violet sphere into the deep. Instead, he wrapped it gently in his handkerchief and placed it securely in his breast pocket, over his heart.

For the first time in his life, the Gleaner walked up the cliffside steps with a secret of his own, leaving the beach a little brighter than he found it.
