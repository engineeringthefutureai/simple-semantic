# The Echoes in Room 4B

In the subterranean vaults of the National Sound Repository, three floors beneath the city pavement, Arthur lived his life in decibels.

His job was quiet work: digitizing fragile magnetic tapes, wax cylinders, and forgotten reel-to-reel recordings before time turned their binder into dust. Surrounded by towering shelves of cataloged tape reels and the low, comforting hum of vacuum-tube preamps, Arthur felt safe. Sound was manageable. Sound was clean. It lived inside calibrated meters and spectral displays.

Until he loaded Tape Reel *74-B-891*.

The box was a plain cardboard square, faded by decades, labeled in smudged black marker: *1974 — Room 4B — Acoustic Baseline Test.* Room 4B had been an anechoic isolation chamber built in the basement during the Cold War, abandoned in the nineties, and demolished during the 2010 library expansion.

Arthur adjusted his heavy monitor headphones, wiped a speck of lint from the playback head, and pressed *Play*.

The soft, rhythmic *tick-tock* of tape hiss filled his ears, followed by the faint hum of air conditioning from fifty years ago. For three minutes, there was nothing but baseline hum—the sterile silence of an empty room in 1974\.

Then came a shift.

It wasn't physical sound at first; it was a drop in static, like a pressure change in an elevator. Through the analog noise, two voices materialized with unsettling, spatial clarity.

"He hasn't noticed the timestamp yet," a woman's voice said. Her voice sounded crisp, unfiltered, as if she were standing three inches from Arthur’s left ear.

Arthur paused. He checked the tape counter. The audio was analog, single-track magnetic. There were no multiple layers.

A second voice, a man with a slight, dry raspy cough, answered. "Give him a second. He's slow. Always checks the reel alignment twice."

Arthur’s hand froze over the tape deck. He *had* checked the reel alignment twice before pressing play. A habit born of obsessive-compulsive discipline.

He lowered the headphones to his neck, staring into the dark corridor of the archives. The room was silent save for the hum of the server racks. He pulled the headphones back over his ears.

"—wearing the green cardigan his sister gave him for his forty-second birthday," the woman said softly. "The one with the loose button on the left cuff."

Arthur looked down at his sleeve. The green wool. The loose thread dangling from the left cuff.

Cold sweat prickled across his collarbone.

"How is this recorded?" Arthur whispered into the empty room, his voice raspy. "Who is this?"

The headphones hissed. On the tape, the man laughed—a low, sympathetic chuckle. "He's asking who we are, Clara. He thinks it's a prank. He doesn't realize Room 4B wasn't built to absorb sound. It was built to retain it."

"Not retain, Arthur," the woman’s voice whispered, using his name for the first time. The sound carried a subtle, impossible echo, as though she were speaking from inside a vast, hollow sphere. "Room 4B was an acoustic focal point. Sound travels across time the way light travels across space. You're not listening to a recording of 1974."

"You're listening to 2058," the man said. "From the very spot where your desk is sitting right now."

Arthur stood up so fast his chair rolled backward, slamming into a shelf of metal tape canisters. He reached for the stop button on the deck.

"Don't press stop, Arthur," Clara’s voice pleaded, her tone suddenly urgent. "If you stop the reel before the track ends, you sever the acoustic bridge. And if you sever the bridge, you'll never hear the warning."

His finger hovered a millimeter above the heavy chrome toggle. "What warning?"

"The lock on the vault door behind you," the man said, his voice dropping to a tense, solemn whisper. "The electronic latch failure at 2:14 AM. The fire suppression system cycling on by mistake."

Arthur turned slowly.

At the end of the long basement aisle, the thick steel vault door clanked. The digital LED display on the keypad flickered from green to a dark, static red.

ERROR: SYSTEM LOCKDOWN.

The lights in the aisle died instantly, plunging the archives into pitch blackness, save for the faint green glow of the tape deck’s VU meters.

Through the headphones, the tape kept spinning. *Click. Whir. Click.*

"Now," Clara said, her voice right beside his ear, so close he could feel the ghost of a breath against his cheek. "Take off the headphones, Arthur."

Arthur reached up with trembling hands and pulled the headphones off.

In the absolute, pitch-black silence of the locked vault, without headphones, without the tape playing... he heard her finish the sentence aloud in the dark room beside him.
