# The Unmetered Moss

In the lowest residential trench of Sub-Sector Seven, light was measured in red digital credit-dits.

Every ceiling fixture carried a steel housing stamped with the seal of the Sol-Ration Authority. To activate the overhead fiber-optic tube for an hour, Mara had to press her biometric wrist-pad to the meter and watch twelve credits drain from her weekly labor allowance. Most nights, she preferred the dark. Darkness was cold, but dark was free.

She sat on the edge of her metal cot, nursing a bowl of reconstituted protein porridge, watching her meter flash its final warning: 00:03 MINUTES REMAINING.

When the fiber line shut off with a hollow, mechanical *clunk*, the room sank into pitch blackness. Above her, six miles of reinforced concrete, basalt, and abandoned subterranean foundations sealed the three hundred thousand residents of Sub-Sector Seven away from the burnt, irradiated surface of Earth.

Mara didn't reach for her credit pad to extend the timer. Instead, she slipped off her heavy work boots and crawled beneath her bed, reaching into the damp space beside the main moisture-collection pipe.

There, resting inside a cracked porcelain sink basin she’d salvaged from the abandoned drainage works, was her secret.

She pulled back a damp burlap sack. A soft, golden glow immediately spilled out, washing over the dark concrete walls of her sleeping alcove.

It was moss—a rare, mutated strain of *Bryum argenteum* she had discovered growing near a ruptured geothermal steam vent in the deepest, unmonitored utility shafts. Traditional subterranean bioluminescent flora emitted a cold, sickly blue tint, barely bright enough to read by. But this strain was different.

It didn't absorb energy from artificial light. It synthesized its glow directly from ambient heat and nitrogen waste in the damp air.

And its light wasn't blue. It was a rich, butter-yellow luminescence that radiated physical, comforting warmth—the exact color temperature of a September afternoon on the surface, recorded in pre-Collapse textbooks.

Mara gently stroked the cushion of velvet green. The heat radiating from the foliage warmed her palm, chasing away the bone-deep chill that clung to everyone in the lower sectors.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

A heavy boot slammed against her reinforced steel door.

"Sol-Ration Inspection\! Open Sector Door 40-B\!" a harsh voice barked through the comm-grille.

Mara’s heart slammed into her ribs. She shoved the burlap sack over the glowing sink basin, slid it under the frame, and scrambled to her feet.

She opened the heavy latch. Standing in the corridor were two inspectors from the Authority, clad in grey composite armor. Their shoulders were pinned with chrome badges depicting a stylized rising sun. The lead inspector held a thermal scanning tablet in his gloved hand.

"Unscheduled sweep, Citizen Mara," the inspector said, pushing past her into the small room. He looked up at the darkened fiber-optic light tube, then down at his tablet. "Grid reports zero power consumption for your unit over the last seventy-two hours."

"I... I sleep early," Mara said, keeping her voice even. "I save my credits for weekend heating."

The inspector narrowed his eyes. He slowly swept his tablet across the small room. "The ambient heat signature in this unit is three degrees higher than the sector average. Unregistered power draws are a Class-Two treason offense against the Sol-Ration Grid."

He paced toward the cot. The sensor on his tablet began to chime—a low, rhythmic beep that grew faster as he approached the bed.

"Under the cot," the officer snapped, drawing an electric stun baton from his belt. "Step back against the wall."

Mara backed away, her hands trembling against the cool metal wall paneling.

The officer knelt down, hooked the end of his baton under the bedframe, and dragged the porcelain sink basin out into the middle of the floor. He pulled back the burlap cover.

The small room was instantly bathed in a blinding, beautiful wave of golden light.

The second officer at the doorway gasped, lowering his guard. The lead inspector froze, his hand suspended in mid-air above his weapon.

For five long seconds, neither man moved.

The harsh, aggressive glare of their visor displays reflected the gentle, sunlike warmth illuminating the dingy concrete walls. The lead officer took a slow, involuntary breath. For the first time in his life, he was smelling the damp, rich aroma of living green earth and warm ozone.

"What... what is this?" the lead officer whispered, his voice losing its rigid authority.

"It’s light," Mara said softly, stepping forward. "Real light. Unmetered. Unowned."

The officer stared at the lush, glowing cushion of moss. He reached out with a trembling gloved finger, touching the soft fronds. He pulled back his glove. The warmth touched his bare skin.

"If the District Manager sees this..." the officer began, his eyes wide with a mix of awe and terror. "They'll burn the whole block. They'll purge the air ducts."

He looked up at Mara, then back down at the glowing basin. The second officer stepped into the room, quietly sliding the heavy steel door shut behind him, sealing the three of them inside the golden sanctuary.

"Does it... does it spread easily?" the second officer asked quietly.

Mara reached into her pocket and pulled out a small glass jar filled with tiny, dust-like spores.

"It thrives in damp air vents," Mara whispered, offering the jar. "It grows anywhere people are cold."

The lead officer looked at the jar for a long, silent moment. Then, with deliberate slowness, he reached up to his tablet, accessed the inspection log, and tapped the interface.

SECTOR 40-B INSPECTION COMPLETE. NO GRID ANOMALIES DETECTED.

He looked up at Mara, his expression grim but resolute. "We inspect Sector Eight tomorrow. The lower ventilation shafts down there haven't been cleaned in ten years."

He took the small jar of spores from her hands, tucked it securely inside his armored breastplate, and stepped out into the dark, silent corridor.
